RFE mobile, redesigned: Nameplate

Your computers are machines you own, so the app reads like the label on a machine: plain, legible and exact. It takes its life from the desktop app: yellow folders, real file and computer objects, a graphite main action like the app icon, and status dots that mean something.

Same product as the desktopFigtree and JetBrains Mono, the desktop’s graphite, taken from the app icon, for the one main action, selection and progress, and its grey selected rows with a fine border.
Things have colour, actions don’tFolders, files, computers, phones and places use the desktop’s Fluent flat emoji, so a folder is yellow and a film is a film strip. Buttons and tools stay as duotone line icons.
A computer has a nameplateIts picture, its name, a status dot and a word for the state. The lamp is never the only signal.
Names keep their endsLong names are cut in the middle, so “…draft v7 (Leila’s comments).docx” keeps the version and the type.
Flat, not cardedRows sit on the page. Sheets appear only for a short choice. There are no cards anywhere.
Three corner sizes16 for sheets that slide in, 8 for anything you press, 3 to 4 for files and photos.
One graphite buttonOne filled button per screen, raised like the desktop’s. Others are outlined or plain text. Red text only for delete, block and forget.
The product is the pictureColour and detail go into what RFE actually shows: the computer’s own pairing window, the 8-digit number both screens must match, a spreadsheet, a waveform and a photo. The chrome around them stays grey.
Three places, labelledComputers, This phone, Transfers. Settings and owner tools sit in the computer’s menu, where they apply.

Removed in the subtraction pass

Things that were drawn, then taken out because they did not help anyone do anything.

  • A storage bar on the home screen. Free space is one line of text on the drive that has it.
  • A floating “+” button. Sending files belongs to a folder, so it lives in the empty folder and the selection bar.
  • A fourth and fifth tab. Search, Shared links and Settings are not places you visit; they are reached from where they apply.
  • Unread-style count badges. The Transfers tab shows a plain number beside its label, in the text colour.
  • Welcome carousel. First run is one screen with one button.
  • “Stop after one download” on share links. The agent does not offer it; the mockup only shows what RFE can do.
  • An empty first-run screen. It now shows what RFE looks like on the computer, so you know what to look for before you walk over.
  • Bold everywhere. Titles dropped from 600 to 500; size and colour carry the hierarchy.
  • Tight tracking on small text and wide mono punctuation. Only the digits are monospaced now.
  • Addresses, ports and fingerprints. Kept, but under a closed “Technical details”.
